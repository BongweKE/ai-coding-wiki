> **Section 13 · Team & Process** — Issues, boards, reviewing AI-written code, token economics, ownership.

*6 lessons.*

## By the end of this section you can

- Run issue-driven development with acceptance criteria an agent can implement against.
- Review AI-written code for the failure modes it actually has.
- Budget tokens and set spend limits before the bill arrives.
- Assign ownership of the paths that matter — including CI configuration itself.

### [Issue-Driven Development](13-Issue-Driven-Development)
`beginner` · ~15 min — An issue is the cheapest review you will ever do. It happens before a branch exists, it takes ten minutes, and it catches the expensive class of mistake: building the wrong thing well.

### [Project Boards And Sprints](13-Project-Boards-And-Sprints)
`beginner` · ~15 min — A board answers one question at a glance: what is actually moving, and what is stuck.

### [Code Review For AI Code](13-Code-Review-For-AI-Code)
`intermediate` · ~20 min — An agent can produce in twenty minutes a diff that would take you a day to write and two hours to review. That is not a bargain if you skim it.

### [Token Economics And Budgets](13-Token-Economics-And-Budgets)
`intermediate` · ~18 min — Token discipline keeps one agent session cheap. Budgets keep a product alive — the difference between a feature whose cost you understand and one that quietly becomes your largest line item.

### [Team Ownership And CODEOWNERS](13-Team-Ownership-CODEOWNERS)
`intermediate` · ~12 min — Ownership answers two questions that otherwise get resolved after an incident: who reviews this? and who is accountable if it breaks?

### [Process Exercises](13-Process-Exercises)
`beginner` · ~20 min — You have read about issues, boards, review, ownership and cost. Reading is not practice.
